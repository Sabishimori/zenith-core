**Purpose:** A single page that presents the Zenith concept to people deciding whether to back it.
**Not:** the product's official marketing site. Nothing here should imply the app is live.

## 1. Why the current page doesn't work for this audience

| Problem | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Shaped like a product launch page ("Create your identity") | Implies the app is live. Investors will try to sign up and find nothing. |
| Feature list, not an argument | Six modules described in parallel. Nothing tells the reader *why this wins*. |
| No business model, market, stage, team, or ask | The four things every backer looks for are all absent. |
| CTA is "Create your identity" | Wrong action entirely. The action here is "back this / talk to us." |
| Generic SaaS shape: hero → problem → features → CTA | Reads like every other startup page, so it competes on polish instead of on idea. |

---

## 2. The fix: an argument, not a brochure

The page is structured as a **concept dossier** — each section answers the reader's *next* question, in the order they'd actually ask it. This is the spine:

```
There is a huge, existing behaviour        →  people already play together daily
Something real is broken in it             →  they meet, then lose each other
Nobody has fixed it, and here's why        →  ← the section most pitches skip
Therefore, here is our insight             →  the relationship is the product
This is what we're building                →  the six parts
This is how value moves through it         →  the coin economy
This is how it becomes a business          →  revenue model  [NEEDS INPUT]
This is who it's for, and why now          →  market + timing
This is honestly where we are              →  stage + roadmap
This is what we're asking for              →  the ask
```

Section 3 ("why nobody fixed it") is the persuasive core. Most pitches jump straight from problem to solution and lose the reader who's thinking *"so why hasn't Discord done this?"*

## 3. Section-by-section

Each entry: **what it must achieve** → *content* → `source`.

### § Opening
**Achieve:** In five seconds, the reader knows what this is and that it's a concept being built.
- The mark, presented as an emblem — not as an app icon.
- One-line thesis. Not the tagline. Something like: *"Multiplayer games are very good at finding you a match, and terrible at helping you keep the people in it."*
- A single honest status line: **"Concept in development — seeking [backers / partners / investment]."** This one line does enormous credibility work.
- Two actions: *Read the concept* (scroll) and *Get in touch*.
- `source: brief + [NEEDS INPUT: stage]`

### § 1 — The behaviour that already exists
**Achieve:** Establish that this is a large, real, existing market — not a habit we have to create.
- Multiplayer play is routine and social. Discord communities, LFG posts, coaching, squads — people are already doing all of this, badly, across scattered tools.
- **Restraint:** no invented statistics. If you supply a real, citable figure, one goes here. Otherwise this argues qualitatively, which is still persuasive.
- `source: brief §The Problem + [NEEDS INPUT: market figures, optional]`

### § 2 — What's broken
**Achieve:** Make the pain specific and felt, not a bulleted list of grievances.
- Five frictions, written as observations rather than complaints:
  unreliable teammates · language and region walls · toxic queues · conversation split across four apps · skill and effort that earn nothing.
- Lead with the emotional one: *a good run ends and there's no thread back to the people in it.*
- `source: brief §The Problem Zenith Solves`

### § 3 — Why this hasn't been solved  ⭐ new section
**Achieve:** Pre-empt "why hasn't Discord/Riot done this?" — the first question any serious reader asks.
- Games optimise for the *match*. Their job ends at the results screen.
- Discord solves the conversation, but only *after* you already know who to talk to. It has no discovery layer.
- LFG tools solve the single session, not the relationship.
- Coaching marketplaces monetise skill but ignore community.
- **Nobody owns the space between matches.** That's the gap.
- `source: derived from brief — this is analysis, flag anything you disagree with`

### § 4 — The insight
**Achieve:** One memorable, defensible idea the reader repeats to a colleague.
- *Games create matches. Communities create memories.* — this line earns its place **here**, as the thesis, not as decorative pull-quote.
- The relationship between players is the durable asset. Zenith makes that relationship the product, and gives it an economy.
- `source: brief §Community Philosophy`

### § 5 — What we're building
**Achieve:** Show the concept is thought-through and complete, without turning into a feature dump.
- Six parts, grouped so they read as a **system**, not a checklist:
  - **Who you are** → Identity
  - **Who you find** → Discovery
  - **Where you stay** → Community / voice
  - **What you can offer** → Player rental & creator collaboration
  - **What you earn** → Zenith Coins
  - **What you spend it on** → Cryptic Stickers & gifts
- The grouping into *you → them → together → value* is the argument. It shows the loop closes.
- `source: brief §Core Experience, §Product Ecosystem`

### § 6 — How value circulates
**Achieve:** Show the economy is a designed loop, not a points gimmick.
- Earn (participation, hosting, helping, tips, events) → Spend (stickers, gifts, creator support, seasonal items) → which rewards the people who make the community good → which produces more of the behaviour that earns.
- Emphasise the design principle: **rewards generosity, not grind.** That's a genuine differentiator and worth stating as a deliberate choice.
- `source: brief §Earn Zenith Coins, §Exchange & Rewards Economy`

### § 7 — How Zenith makes money  🔴 BLOCKED
**Achieve:** Answer the question every investor asks. **Currently unanswerable from the brief.**
- Candidate models to choose from — pick, and I'll write it:
  - Commission on player-rental / coaching bookings
  - Coin sales (players buy coins as well as earn them)
  - Marketplace cut on gifts and creator support
  - Premium subscription (enhanced discovery, profile, cosmetics)
  - Seasonal / collectible sales
- Until you decide, this section renders as an **explicitly labelled open question**. That is far better than silence *and* far better than a guess — it reads as intellectual honesty, and investors respond well to "here are the three models we're testing."
- `source: [NEEDS INPUT — highest priority]`

### § 8 — Who it's for
**Achieve:** Show a real wedge audience, not "all gamers."
- Primary: multiplayer players, ranked players, Discord communities, esports followers.
- Secondary (the supply side): creators, streamers, coaches, organisers, tournament hosts.
- **Add the argument the brief implies but doesn't state:** this is a two-sided market. Creators bring their communities; communities give creators a reason to stay. Naming that is investor-legible.
- `source: brief §Target Audience + analysis`

### § 9 — Why now
**Achieve:** Timing. Why is this possible/necessary today rather than five years ago.
- Community-first play has moved to the centre of gaming culture.
- Creators already act as community anchors, with no purpose-built home.
- Players increasingly expect their participation to be worth something.
- `source: derived — confirm you're comfortable with these claims`

### § 10 — Where we are  🔴 NEEDS INPUT
**Achieve:** Honesty that builds trust. Concept-stage is credible; vagueness is not.
- Plain statement of what exists today: brand and product concept defined, [designs / prototype / nothing yet].
- Short roadmap with phases rather than fake dates: Concept → Prototype → Closed alpha → Community beta.
- `source: [NEEDS INPUT: stage + roadmap]`

### § 11 — The team  🔴 NEEDS INPUT
**Achieve:** Who's behind it. On Kickstarter this is often the deciding factor.
- Names, roles, one line of relevant background each.
- If it's a solo founder, say so — that's fine and common.
- `source: [NEEDS INPUT]`

### § 12 — The ask
**Achieve:** Tell the reader exactly what to do next.
- Swaps by audience (decision #1):

## 4. Layout reference — kononenkogroup.com

**Site:** Kononenko Architectural Bureau. Nuxt build, Lenis smooth-scroll, self-hosted fonts.
Measured live at 1280×720. Every number below is taken from the running page, not estimated.

### 4.1 The measured system

**Grid — 15 columns.**

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Page margin | **20px** each side (edge-to-edge, not a centred container) |
| Columns | **15** × 70.22px |
| Gutter | **13.33px** |
| Content width | 1240px at 1280 viewport |
| Column pitch | 83.55px |

Everything snaps to it. The recurring left edges — 20 / 354 / 438 / 605 / 688 / 856 / 1190px — are exactly columns 1, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 15. The recurring block widths are 1-col (70px), 2-col (154px), 3-col (237px), 7-col (571px), and full-bleed (1240px).

15 columns is the whole trick. 12 columns pushes you toward halves and thirds, which read as "template." 15 is an odd number that resists symmetry, so every composition lands *off-centre on purpose*. That single choice is most of why the page feels designed rather than assembled.

**Type — one weight for the entire site.**

There is no bold anywhere. Every piece of text is weight 400. All hierarchy comes from *size*, and the jumps are enormous.

| px | Role | Tracking | Leading | Instances |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 117 | Display / section openers | −3.5px | 0.70–0.85 | 16 |
| 79 | Large statements | −2.36px | 0.90 | 19 |
| 53 | Mid headings | −1.6px | 0.80–0.88 | 12 |
| 29 | Rare accent | −0.59px | 1.21 | 1 |
| 27 | Body / lede | −0.8px | **1.09** | 22 |
| 13 | Small | −0.25px | 0.97 | 6 |
| **11** | **Labels, nav, captions, metadata** | −0.21px | 1.16 | **169** |
| 8 | Micro numerals (01–08) | −0.21px | 1.20 | 8 |

Two observations that matter more than the numbers:

1. **Tracking is −0.030em at every single size.** One systematic rule, applied everywhere.
2. **Leading is tight to the point of aggression.** Display sits at 0.70–0.85 — lines physically overlap their own box. Even body runs at 1.09, roughly half of a normal 1.6. Text reads as a *block of material*, which is exactly the architectural feeling.
3. **11px is the workhorse** — 169 of ~280 text elements. Tiny type used confidently everywhere, against 117px display. That extreme ratio (roughly 11:1) is the page's signature.

**Two typefaces, both grotesques, mixed inside the same headline.**
`n.woff2` is slightly condensed (7% narrower than Helvetica); `h.woff2` is wide (7% wider). They alternate *within* a single line — "Kononenko" set in one, "Bureau" in the other, at identical size. It reads as texture rather than as two fonts.

**Colour — three values.**

| Value | Use |
|---|---|
| `#000000` | Text, and the entire final third of the page |
| `#FFFFFF` | Ground, and text on black |
| `#929292` | Secondary text — the *only* tint |
| `#F8F8F8` | One barely-there panel |

No accent colour at all. **Border-radius is `0` everywhere**, with a single exception: `50%` circles.

**Total: 23.2 screens.** Only 1,035 DOM nodes and 27 media items — long but extremely lean.

### 4.3 The eight moves worth stealing

1. **15-column grid** — forces asymmetry, kills the template feel.
2. **One font weight.** Hierarchy from size alone. Removes every "just bold it" decision.
3. **Extreme size ratio.** 11px against 117px, with almost nothing between.
4. **Tracking as one rule** (−0.03em) at every size.
5. **Sub-1.0 leading on display type.** Headlines become blocks.
6. **20px page margins.** Content runs to the edge; the *interior* holds the white space.
7. **The mid-page inversion to black.** One structural event carries the whole back half.
8. **Radius 0, except circles.** Nothing is softened.

### 4.4 Translating it to Zenith — honest assessment

| Move | Verdict for Zenith |
|---|---|
| 15-col grid, 20px margins | **Adopt directly.** |
| One weight, size-only hierarchy | **Adopt.** Biggest single upgrade to the current page. |
| 11px ↔ huge display ratio | **Adopt**, floor at 12px for accessibility on a page investors read on phones. |
| −0.03em tracking rule | **Adopt.** |
| Tight leading | **Adopt on display.** Body goes to ~1.5, not 1.09 — 27px/1.09 works for a bureau's four sentences, not for a page with an actual argument to make. |
| Radius 0 | **Adopt.** Sharp corners suit a dossier and fight the "app landing page" read. |
| Mid-page inversion | **Adopt** — invert at the pitch turn (§7 model / §10 stage / §12 ask). |
| Monochrome | **Adapt.** Keep the near-monochrome discipline, but the violet is your brand — spend it in *one or two places only*, most likely the mark and the single action. |
| Pinned 4–5 screen scroll sequences | **Reject.** They exist to display architectural renders. We have no imagery, and an investor skimming on a phone should never be trapped in a pinned section. |
| 23-screen length | **Reject.** Target **8–11 screens.** A bureau is browsed; a pitch is assessed. |

### 4.5 🔴 The real problem: they have photographs, we don't

Their layout is load-bearing on 27 large architectural renders. Strip those out and the composition is mostly empty. **This is the one thing that will not transfer**, and it needs a decision:

| Option | Notes |
|---|---|
| **A — Typographic** | Let huge type and the grid carry it. Zero images. Cheapest, fastest, genuinely credible for a concept dossier. **My recommendation.** |
| **B — UI mockups** | Design 3–4 app screens as the imagery. Strongest for investors — shows the product is real in your head. Significant extra work. |
| **C — The mark as recurring motif** | Use the axolotl at large scale as the visual event. Distinctive and on-brand, but thin if repeated. |
| **D — Abstract diagrams** | Draw the coin loop and the two-sided market as real geometry. Suits investors, earns its place, no faking. Good paired with A. |

**Recommended: A + D.** Typography carries the page; two or three real diagrams provide the visual beats. Add B later if you want to strengthen the investor case.

## 5. Design direction (revised against the reference)

**Thesis: a concept dossier, not a landing page** — built on the Kononenko system, in Zenith's palette.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Grid** | 15 columns, 13.33px gutters, 20px page margins. Compose off-centre. |
| **Type scale** | Display 96–128px · statement 64–80px · heading 40–53px · body 18–20px · label 12px. One weight throughout. |
| **Tracking** | −0.03em at every size. |
| **Leading** | 0.85 display · 1.5 body (deliberate departure — we have real reading to do). |
| **Faces** | Two grotesques, mixed within headlines, per the reference. Instrument Serif is now the wrong call — it was chosen for a product page. |
| **Colour** | Near-monochrome: pearl `#FBF8FF` ground, ink `#120A24`, grey secondary. Violet `#6B3FD1` reserved for the mark and the single action. |
| **Radius** | `0`. Circles only where a circle is meant. |
| **Inversion** | Page flips to deep violet-black at the pitch turn and stays there through the ask. |
| **Section numbers** | Earned — the argument is a genuine sequence. Set at 12px against large headings. |
| **Motion** | Restrained. Lenis-style smooth scroll is fine; no pinned sequences. |
| **Length** | 8–11 screens. |
| **Placeholders** | Visibly marked, never disguised as finished content. |

### Avoid
- Card grids as page structure
- Any metric, logo, testimonial, or figure that isn't real
- "Sign up" / "Download" / "Create your identity" — the product isn't live
- Countdown timers, fake urgency, fake social proof
- Pinned scroll sections
- Feature-listing where an argument belongs
